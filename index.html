Why do we even use a methodology?

A data science methodology gives a structured workflow so we can move from raw data to insights and deployable results, while staying organized, consistent, and iterative.

  • Structured: clear phases and steps
  • Iterative: loop back and refine
  • Adaptable: fits different projects

These ideas are summarized in the Week 8 notes under “Data Science Life Cycle”. (Paraphrased.)

Universal lifecycle (big picture)

Different frameworks use different names, but they share the same “shape”: Understand → Gather Data → Prepare → Model → Evaluate → Apply.

CRISP-DM matches this universal flow by adding strong focus on business goals and deployment, which is why it’s often used in industry projects.

Quick compare (CRISP-DM vs OSEMN vs KDD)

Based on the Week 8 comparison table, here’s the short version in my own words:

Method Main vibe Best fit Common limitation
CRISP-DM Business-first, end-to-end Industry deployments Feels heavy for tiny projects
OSEMN Hands-on coding workflow EDA + student ML projects No explicit business/deploy phase
KDD Pattern discovery focus Academic / mining large datasets Less emphasis on business/deploy

These points summarize commonly accepted differences between data science methodologies.

Key Takeaways

  • Goal of a methodology: provide a structured workflow for data projects.
  • OSEMN: Obtain, Scrub, Explore, Model, Notify.
  • KDD focus: pattern discovery from data.
  • Most widely used in industry: CRISP-DM for end-to-end projects.
  • Common pattern across all: modeling and evaluation always appear.
